\begin{abstract}
Flat \LCDM is an exceptionally successful empirical model, but it leaves the observed vacuum component as a rigid parameter rather than an explained infrared quantity. This paper reformulates R-Universe around one central claim: the measured late-time vacuum term is the homogeneous infrared attractor of an internally determined relational scale, not a primitive cosmological constant and not merely an arbitrary dark-energy equation of state. The work is deliberately split into two levels. Level I is the fully reproducible late-time background limit tested here. Level II is the proposed physical completion: a causal relational scale \kR determined by past geometry and stress history, whose nonlocal memory should link expansion, structure growth, gravitational slip and standard-siren propagation. The Level-I branch R1 is defined by
\[
E^2(z)-(1-\Omega_{\rm m0})E^{2-\theta}(z)-\Omega_{\rm m0}(1+z)^3=0,
\]
and contains flat \LCDM exactly and regularly at $\theta=2$. A second stress branch R2,
\[
E^2(z)-(1-\Omega_{\rm m0})(1+z)^\nu E^{2-\theta}(z)-\Omega_{\rm m0}(1+z)^3=0,
\]
tests whether additional shape freedom is statistically justified. The calculated chain includes the homogeneous background dynamics, supernova and BAO distances, profiled supernova intercepts, full-covariance DESI BAO likelihoods, cosmic chronometers, an alternative DES-Dovekie supernova-pipeline cross-check, numerical validation, profile likelihood in $\theta$, dataset robustness tests, a free-$r_d$ stress test, a direct $w$CDM/CPL competitor audit, an absorption-branch scan, a Laplace posterior/evidence proxy, parameter-free R$\alpha$ master predictions, and a reference \LCDM CAMB baseline. For Pantheon+ full covariance + DESI DR2 BAO + cosmic chronometers, R1 gives $\Delta\chi^2=-3.7119$, $\Delta{\rm AIC}=-1.7119$ and $\Delta{\rm BIC}=+3.6808$ relative to \LCDM. A stricter absorption branch, R$\alpha$, uses $\theta(a)=2-\alpha a$, so that \LCDM is recovered exactly at $\alpha=0$ and also as $a\rightarrow0$. It gives $\Delta\chi^2=-4.5008$, $\Delta{\rm AIC}=-2.5008$ and $\Delta{\rm BIC}=+2.8918$, slightly lower AIC than the same-likelihood $w$CDM value $\Delta{\rm AIC}=-2.3155$. The Laplace approximation gives $\alpha=0.497\pm0.209$ and prior-dependent $\ln B_{{\rm R}\alpha,\Lambda{\rm CDM}}$ from $-0.36$ to $+1.25$; after the five-branch look-elsewhere correction the background preference has $p\simeq0.158$, so it is a preregisterable signal rather than a discovery. The new falsifiable content is the minimal relational-kernel closure: the same $\alpha$ predicts $\Xi(1)=1.0951$, $\mu(0.01\,h{\rm Mpc}^{-1},1)=1.000712$, $\eta(0.01\,h{\rm Mpc}^{-1},1)=0.999644$, and the master relation $(\eta-1)/(\mu-1)=-1/2$ without additional fitted parameters. If this single-kernel relation fails in joint growth, lensing and standard-siren data, the minimal R$\alpha$ completion fails. If it holds, \LCDM becomes the frozen local limit of a broader infrared-scale dynamics.

\section{Introduction}

Its success, however, does not explain why the vacuum sector should be a perfectly rigid cosmological constant. The cosmological constant problem remains a structural problem: a constant shift of the matter Lagrangian changes the stress tensor by a metric-proportional vacuum term and should gravitate in ordinary local Einstein equations \citep{Weinberg1989}.

R-Universe is tested here as a conservative and falsifiable late-time background extension. It does not assume that local general relativity fails in tested regimes. Instead, it asks whether the cosmological vacuum term entering the homogeneous infrared background can be represented by an effective infrared scaling law motivated by global constraints or renormalization-group flow. The model is deliberately constructed so that \LCDM is recovered exactly at the homogeneous background level. \section{Physical principle}

\subsection{Vacuum energy as an infrared observable}

The physical premise is not that arbitrary model flexibility should be added to \LCDM. It is that the observed vacuum component may be an infrared observable rather than the raw microscopic vacuum energy. Vacuum-energy sequestering frameworks show how global variables and constraints can remove sensitivity of local curvature to additive vacuum-energy shifts \citep{KaloperPadilla2014,KaloperPadilla2015}. R-Universe uses this as motivation, not as a completed derivation: the empirical model below is self-contained and can be falsified without assuming a unique microscopic sequestering implementation.

\subsection{Relational scale instead of external cutoff}

The new physical claim is not the algebraic background curve by itself. Similar homogeneous histories can arise from interacting vacuum or decomposed Chaplygin-type descriptions \citep{Wands2012,WangWands2013}. The proposed R-Universe novelty is the scale-setting rule behind the curve. A universe with no external clock or ruler should not receive its infrared scale from outside the system. The scale should be determined by internal causal relations:
\begin{equation}
\kR(x)=\mathcal K[g_{\mu\nu},T_{\mu\nu};J^-(x)].
\label{eq:krel}
\end{equation}
Here $J^-(x)$ is the causal past of the event $x$. The simplest mathematical skeleton is a causal memory operator,
\begin{equation}
\MemR[X](x)=
\frac{
\displaystyle\int_{J^-(x)}\dd^4y\sqrt{-g(y)}\,K_\ell(x,y)X(y)
}{
\displaystyle\int_{J^-(x)}\dd^4y\sqrt{-g(y)}\,K_\ell(x,y)
},
\label{eq:memory}
\end{equation}
where $K_\ell(x,y)$ has support only in the causal past. A stationary relational-scale law can then be written schematically as
\begin{equation}
\frac{\delta\Gamma_{\mathcal R}[g_{\mu\nu},\Psi,\kR,\MemR]}{\delta\ln\kR(x)}=0.
\label{eq:kstationary}
\end{equation}
Equation~\eqref{eq:kstationary} is not yet a complete action. It is the target that a fundamental R-Universe completion must meet. It also explains why the purely local ansatz $k\sim H$ is insufficient as a final principle: it erases the causal history that \kR is supposed to encode.

\subsection{Infrared attractor}

In a spatially flat homogeneous limit, $\Theta=\nabla_\mu u^\mu=3H$ and a sharply localized memory kernel gives $\kR\simeq H$. If the running component scales as $\rho_{\mathcal R}\propto\kR^{2-\theta}$, then
\begin{equation}
\Omega_{\mathcal R}=\frac{\rho_{\mathcal R}}{\rho_{\rm tot}}
\propto\frac{H^{2-\theta}}{H^2}
\propto H^{-\theta},
\end{equation}
so the invariant
\begin{equation}
H\Omega_{\mathcal R}^{1/\theta}=I_0
\label{eq:ir-attractor}
\end{equation}
emerges in the local homogeneous infrared limit. The operational Level-I model tested in this paper is the late-time background realization of Eq.~\eqref{eq:ir-attractor}. The important shift is conceptual: the invariant is not treated as a primitive axiom, but as the candidate attractor of a deeper causal scale law.

Define the attractor mismatch
\begin{equation}
\mathcal A =
\ln\frac{H}{H_0}
+\frac{1}{\theta}\ln\frac{\Omega_{\mathcal R}}{\Omega_{\mathcal R0}}.
\end{equation}
A minimal well-posed completion should contain a relaxation equation of the form
\begin{equation}
u^\mu\nabla_\mu\mathcal A
=-\Gamma_{\mathcal R}\mathcal A
+\epsilon_{\rm mem}\mathcal S_{\rm mem},
\qquad
\Gamma_{\mathcal R}>0,
\label{eq:attractor-relaxation}
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal S_{\rm mem}$ vanishes in the local homogeneous limit. This is a falsifiable mathematical requirement: if no covariant, stable, causal theory can generate Eq.~\eqref{eq:attractor-relaxation}, the Level-II R-Universe does not exist.

\subsection{Renormalization-group scaling}

In asymptotic-safety and functional-renormalization approaches, the effective gravitational action depends on a coarse-graining scale $k$ \citep{Reuter1998,ReuterSaueressig2019,BonannoSaueressig2017,Platania2020}. A dimensionless cosmological coupling $\lambda(k)=\Lambda(k)/k^2$ linearized around a fixed point satisfies schematically
\begin{equation}
k\frac{\dd}{\dd k}(\lambda-\lambda_*)=-\theta_{\rm RG}(\lambda-\lambda_*),
\end{equation}
so that
\begin{equation}
\Lambda(k)=\lambda_*k^2+Ck^{2-\theta_{\rm RG}}.
\end{equation}
Identifying the late-time infrared scale with $k\propto H$ motivates an effective component
\begin{equation}
\rho_R(H)=\rho_{R0}E^{2-\theta}.
\label{eq:rhoR}
\end{equation}
This step is an RG-motivated ansatz, not a derivation from a specified ultraviolet completion. The microscopic relation would be
\begin{equation}
\rho_\Lambda(k)=\frac{\Lambda(k)}{8\pi G(k)},
\end{equation}
so any running of $G(k)$ changes both the exponent and normalization. The fixed-point contribution $\lambda_*k^2$ maps to a term proportional to $H^2$ if $k=\xi H$ and can be partially degenerate with the left-hand side of the Friedmann equation or with a running Newton coupling. The present R1 model therefore absorbs these unknowns into the empirical normalization and exponent in Eq.~\eqref{eq:rhoR}. Alternative scale identifications such as $k^2\propto R$, $k\propto t^{-1}$, or $k^2\propto H^2+\alpha\dot H$ are not equivalent and are treated as theoretical systematics rather than completed calculations. The observational exponent $\theta$ is therefore an effective exponent $\theta_{\rm obs}$, not a direct measurement of a stability-matrix eigenvalue $\theta_{\rm RG}$. A future fundamental derivation must specify the truncation, regulator, gauge choice, running of $G$, Bianchi identity implementation, and energy exchange or non-exchange between matter and the R-sector.

\section{Background equations}

Assume a spatially flat late-time background and conserved pressureless matter,
\begin{equation}
\rho_m(z)=\rho_{m0}(1+z)^3.
\end{equation}
Using Eq.~\eqref{eq:rhoR}, the normalized Friedmann equation becomes
\begin{equation}
E^2(z)=\Omega_{\rm m0}(1+z)^3+(1-\Omega_{\rm m0})E^{2-\theta}(z).
\label{eq:r1}
\end{equation}
Equivalently,
\begin{equation}
F(E,z;\Omega_{\rm m0},\theta)=
E^2-(1-\Omega_{\rm m0})E^{2-\theta}
-\Omega_{\rm m0}(1+z)^3=0.
\end{equation}

\subsection{Exact embedding of \LCDM}

For $\theta=2$,
\begin{equation}
E^{2-\theta}=1,
\end{equation}
and Eq.~\eqref{eq:r1} becomes
\begin{equation}
E^2(z)=\Omega_{\rm m0}(1+z)^3+1-\Omega_{\rm m0},
\end{equation}
which is exactly flat \LCDM. Thus
\[
{\cal M}_{\Lambda{\rm CDM}}\subset{\cal M}_{\rm R1}
\]
at the background level. The limit is regular on the physical branch because
\begin{equation}
\frac{\partial F}{\partial E}
=2E-(1-\Omega_{\rm m0})(2-\theta)E^{1-\theta}
\rightarrow 2E>0
\end{equation}
at $\theta=2$ for $E>0$. The numerical solver therefore follows the positive branch connected to \LCDM. This is implemented in \codepath{code/extended_fit.py}.

\subsection{\texorpdfstring{\LCDM}{LCDM} absorption principle}

The stronger R-Universe objective is not merely to beat \LCDM by a small distance-likelihood number. It is to make \LCDM appear as the frozen member of a wider infrared-scale dynamics. The minimal absorption requirements are
\begin{align}
{\rm A1:}\quad &\alpha=0 \Rightarrow \mathcal M_{\rm R\alpha}=\mathcal M_{\Lambda{\rm CDM}},\\
{\rm A2:}\quad &a\rightarrow0 \Rightarrow \theta(a)\rightarrow2,\\
{\rm A3:}\quad &a\simeq1 \Rightarrow \theta(a)\neq2\ {\rm only\ if\ late\ data\ demand\ it}.
\end{align}
A minimal one-parameter realization is
\begin{equation}
\theta(a)=2-\alpha a,\qquad a=(1+z)^{-1},
\label{eq:ralpha-theta}
\end{equation}
with
\begin{equation}
E^2(z)=\Omega_{\rm m0}(1+z)^3+
(1-\Omega_{\rm m0})E^{2-\theta(a)}(z).
\label{eq:ralpha}
\end{equation}
This branch, denoted R$\alpha$, absorbs \LCDM in two senses. First, $\alpha=0$ gives the exact flat-\LCDM background for all redshifts. Second, at fixed nonzero $\alpha$, the early limit $a\rightarrow0$ drives $\theta(a)\rightarrow2$, so the branch approaches the standard early-universe expansion rather than perturbing the CMB-calibrated era by construction. The deviation is concentrated at late times, where the public supernova and low-redshift BAO data actually test the vacuum closure.

R$\alpha$ is still a phenomenological background proxy. It does not replace the required Level-II covariant memory kernel. Its purpose is to encode the physical idea that \LCDM is not a rival model bolted beside R-Universe, but a stable zero-running limit inside it.

\label{eq:kappa-kernel}
\end{equation}
The scalar $\mathcal S_R$ is chosen so that the homogeneous local-memory limit tracks the expansion scalar, $\kR\rightarrow H/c$, while the first correction to the frozen \LCDM point is proportional to the scale factor. The important point is not the Taylor series itself, but the hierarchy it imposes: early times and zero running return to \LCDM, while late-time deviations are controlled by one measured amplitude $\alpha$.

Failure of either relation would falsify this minimal Level-II closure even if the background fit remained acceptable.

\subsection{R2 stress branch}

The R2 stress branch is
\begin{equation}
E^2-(1-\Omega_{\rm m0})(1+z)^\nu E^{2-\theta}
-\Omega_{\rm m0}(1+z)^3=0.
\label{eq:r2}

It has one more parameter than R1 and two more than \LCDM. It is not treated as the preferred theory. R2 is therefore not used as an independent physical proposal, not used in the main interpretation and not advertised as an RG prediction. Its only role is methodological: to check whether the R1 improvement is merely the first sign that arbitrary redshift-shape freedom can lower $\chi^2$. Because the answer is penalized by BIC, the discussion below keeps R1 as the only physically motivated branch.

\subsection{Bianchi identity and effective-background status}

Equations~\eqref{eq:rhoR} and \eqref{eq:r1} should be read as an effective metric-background closure, not as a completed covariant stress-energy theory. In general relativity the Bianchi identity implies
\begin{equation}
\nabla^\mu G_{\mu\nu}=0,\qquad
\dot\rho_{\rm tot}+3H(\rho_{\rm tot}+p_{\rm tot})=0.
\end{equation}
If pressureless matter is separately conserved as $\rho_m\propto a^{-3}$ while $\rho_R$ runs with $H$, then a fundamental embedding must explain where the compensating term lives. There are two logically distinct possibilities. One is an interacting dark-sector description,
\begin{align}
\dot\rho_m+3H\rho_m &= Q,\\
\dot\rho_R+3H(\rho_R+p_R) &= -Q,
\end{align}
with $Q=0$ only for the effective background fit used here. The other is a modified-gravity or boundary-condition embedding in which the running component belongs to the geometric side of the field equations rather than to a separately conserved material fluid. The present paper does not derive either completion. It therefore claims only a closed late-time background test of Eq.~\eqref{eq:r1}; it does not claim that the scaling law by itself is a covariantly complete replacement for \LCDM.

\section{Distances and likelihood observables}

The comoving distance is
\begin{equation}
D_C(z)=\frac{c}{H_0}\int_0^z\frac{\dd z'}{E(z')}.
\end{equation}
For a flat background,
\begin{align}
D_M(z)&=D_C(z),\\
D_H(z)&=\frac{c}{H_0E(z)},\\
D_V(z)&=\left[zD_M^2(z)D_H(z)\right]^{1/3}.
\end{align}
All BAO fits fix
\begin{equation}
r_d=147.09\ {\rm Mpc}.
\end{equation}
This is a late-time test. A future complete early-universe model must compute $r_d$.

The fixed sound horizon is a conditional calibration choice, not an R1 prediction. BAO observables measure ratios such as $D_M/r_d$ and $D_H/r_d$, and the calibration of $r_d$ is correlated with early-universe quantities including $\omega_b$, $\omega_c$, radiation, neutrino physics and recombination. Fixing one value therefore suppresses the early--late covariance that a global CMB+BAO fit would need to propagate. The free-$r_d$ test below checks only whether the late-time preference is numerically fragile under a floating scale; it does not replace a self-consistent early-universe R-sector calculation.

For supernovae,
\begin{equation}
\mu_{\rm th}=5\log_{10}(D_L/{\rm Mpc})+25,
\end{equation}
with
\begin{equation}
D_L=(1+z_{\rm hel})D_C(z_{\rm HD})
\end{equation}
for Pantheon+. The absolute magnitude/offset is analytically marginalized:
\begin{equation}
\chi^2_{\rm SN}=r^TC^{-1}r-
\frac{(1^TC^{-1}r)^2}{1^TC^{-1}1}.
\label{eq:snchi}
\end{equation}

BAO and chronometer likelihoods are
\begin{align}
\chi^2_{\rm BAO}&=\Delta_{\rm BAO}^TC_{\rm BAO}^{-1}\Delta_{\rm BAO},\\
\chi^2_{\rm CC}&=\sum_i\left[\frac{H_i-H_0E(z_i)}{\sigma_{H,i}}\right]^2.
\label{eq:baocc}
\end{align}

\section{Conditional subhorizon growth forecast under a smooth R-sector GR closure}

A full perturbation theory requires an action or complete field equations. The present paper therefore states the conditional closure used for a late-time diagnostic calculation:
\begin{enumerate}
\item pressureless matter is conserved and minimally coupled;
\item the R-sector is smooth on the subhorizon scales used in the compressed growth check;
\item $G_{\rm eff}=G$ in the Poisson equation;
\item the background expansion is given by R1 or R2.
\end{enumerate}
Under this closure, matter growth satisfies
\begin{equation}
\frac{\dd^2D}{\dd\ln a^2}
\,+\,
\left[2+\frac{\dd\ln H}{\dd\ln a}\right]\frac{\dd D}{\dd\ln a}
-\frac{3}{2}\Omega_m(a)D=0,
\label{eq:growth}
\end{equation}
where
\begin{equation}
\Omega_m(a)=\frac{\Omega_{\rm m0}a^{-3}}{E^2(a)}.
\end{equation}
The solver starts at $a=10^{-3}$ with $D\propto a$ and normalizes to $D(0)=1$. The main R1 branch gives a maximum suppression of the normalized $fD$ proxy relative to \LCDM of $3.42\%$ at $z\simeq0.33$. This is not a prediction for $f\sigma_8$ until an amplitude parameter such as $\sigma_{8,0}$ or $A_s$ is specified.

This growth calculation is intentionally a GR-background benchmark. It is not the generic perturbation theory of an RG-driven vacuum sector. In a complete RG implementation, a running $\Lambda(k)$ would generically be accompanied by a running or scale-dependent $G(k)$, modified constraint equations and possibly nontrivial R-sector perturbations. The Poisson equation could then involve $G_{\rm eff}(k,z)$, anisotropic stress or a modified Weyl response. Such terms can suppress, erase or amplify the simple $3.42\%$ $fD$ proxy quoted above. The plotted growth and ISW curves are therefore falsifiable bookkeeping diagnostics for the background history, not final predictions for RSD, weak lensing or CMB cross-correlations.

For a CMB-relevant proxy, the minimal GR Weyl-potential scaling is
\begin{equation}
\Phi(z)\propto D(z)(1+z).
\end{equation}
The late ISW source is proportional to its time derivative. The code computes a redshift-derivative proxy and obtains, at $z=1$,
\[
{\cal I}_{\rm R1}=0.1011,\qquad
{\cal I}_{\Lambda{\rm CDM}}=0.0779.
\]
This proxy is kept as a diagnostic only. A real ISW observable requires $k$-dependent Weyl potentials, line-of-sight integration and survey window functions.

\section{Level-II smoking-gun tests}

Background expansion alone is dark-degenerate: many theories can reproduce nearly the same $H(z)$, including interacting vacuum, Chaplygin-type decompositions, $w$CDM and CPL-like phenomenology \citep{Wands2012,WangWands2013,Copeland2006}. The reason R-Universe could become important is not that R1 lowers $\chi^2$ by a few units. It is that a single causal relational scale should control several sectors that are usually parameterized independently.

In Newtonian gauge,
\begin{equation}
\dd s^2=-(1+2\Psi)\dd t^2+a^2(t)(1-2\Phi)\dd{\bf x}^2.
\end{equation}
Define
\begin{equation}
\mu(k,z)=\frac{G_{\rm eff}(k,z)}{G},\qquad
\eta(k,z)=\frac{\Phi(k,z)}{\Psi(k,z)},\qquad
\Xi(z)=\frac{d_L^{\rm GW}(z)}{d_L^{\rm EM}(z)}.
\end{equation}
\LCDM gives $\mu=1$, $\eta=1$ and $\Xi=1$ up to standard small corrections. Generic dark-energy backgrounds can alter $H(z)$ while leaving these functions unrelated or freely parameterized. A Level-II R-Universe completion should instead impose a constrained relation because the same \kR controls vacuum response, effective coupling and tensor propagation friction.

The minimal proposed null combination is
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
\mathcal T_R(k,z)
\equiv
\left[\eta(k,z)-1\right]
+\frac{1}{2}\left[\mu(k,z)-1\right]
-\frac{\dd\ln\Xi(z)}{\dd\ln a}\,\mathcal W(k,z)
=0
}
\label{eq:r-triangle}
\end{equation}
up to higher-order memory terms. The window $\mathcal W(k,z)$ is determined by the same kernel $K_\ell$ that appears in Eq.~\eqref{eq:memory}; in the local homogeneous limit $\mathcal W\to1$. Equation~\eqref{eq:r-triangle} is the cleanest near-term way to make R-Universe more than a background fit. It can be wrong in a measurable way.

\section{Model selection}

Model comparison uses
\begin{equation}
{\rm AIC}=\chi^2+2k,\qquad
{\rm BIC}=\chi^2+k\ln N,
\end{equation}
with $k=2$ for \LCDM, $k=3$ for R1 and $k=4$ for R2 \citep{Akaike1974,Schwarz1978}. Negative $\Delta$ values favor R-Universe relative to \LCDM.

The supernova intercept is common to all compared models. Including it in $k$ would shift all absolute AIC/BIC values equally within a fixed data combination and leave the reported model differences unchanged. The $N$ used in BIC is the dimension of the fitted data vector, e.g. $N=1580+13+31=1624$ for Pantheon+ + DESI DR2 BAO + chronometers. Because the supernova and BAO blocks are correlated, this is an operational BIC convention rather than a true count of statistically independent objects. The BIC values should therefore be read as a standard approximate penalty diagnostic; a nested-sampling evidence calculation is required for final Bayesian model comparison.

\section{Current limitations}

The present release is complete only as a background-level late-time reproducibility study with conditional growth diagnostics. The physical motivation is stated, the background equation is closed, the homogeneous \LCDM limit is exact, the public late-time likelihood observables are computed, data provenance is recorded, profile-likelihood and robustness stress tests are included, and the numerical results are generated by public code.

The following items are not completed calculations in this release:
\begin{enumerate}
\item the RG mapping from $\Lambda(k)$ and $G(k)$ to $\rho_R(H)$ is a motivated ansatz, not a derived theorem;
\item no covariant action or complete field equations for the R-sector are supplied;
\item the separate conservation of pressureless matter together with a running effective $\rho_R(H)$ has not yet been embedded in a covariant Bianchi-consistent theory with an explicit interaction term or modified-gravity boundary sector;
\item $\theta$ is fitted phenomenologically and is not computed from a specified RG stability matrix;
\item no ghost or gradient-stability analysis is performed;
\item the BAO calibration uses a fixed or phenomenologically floated $r_d$ and does not yet propagate the full early--late covariance of $\omega_b$, $\omega_c$, radiation, neutrinos, recombination and drag-epoch physics;
\item the growth equation assumes a smooth R-sector GR closure with $G_{\rm eff}=G$ and $\eta=1$ rather than a derived modified Poisson equation with running $G(k)$;
\item no R1 CLASS/CAMB module, R1 CMB spectra, Planck likelihood or global CMB+BAO+SN+RSD/WL inference is completed;
\item no full MCMC, nested-sampling evidence, posterior covariance matrix or prior-sensitivity scan is yet included.
\item the direct competitor audit in Table~\ref{tab:competitor-audit} shows that $w$CDM currently gives a lower AIC than constant-exponent R1 on the same late-time likelihood;
\item the absorption branch R$\alpha$ in Table~\ref{tab:r-running-scan} improves on $w$CDM in AIC, but flat \LCDM still retains the lowest BIC.
\end{enumerate}
These are not optional for a claim that R-Universe physically replaces or subsumes \LCDM. They are the next falsification gates.

\section{Conclusion}

R-Universe R1 is a closed, reproducible, one-parameter late-time background extension of flat \LCDM. It exactly contains \LCDM at $\theta=2$ at the homogeneous background level, computes all late-time distance observables used here, uses full covariance where available, includes an alternative DES-Dovekie supernova-pipeline cross-check, and gives conditional growth diagnostics under a stated smooth R-sector GR closure. This Level-I result is not presented as the full theory. It is the audit-complete infrared limit of a broader Level-II proposal in which the vacuum closure is generated by a causal relational scale rather than inserted as a rigid constant or arbitrary equation of state.

The empirical result is precise but modest. Pantheon+ full covariance + DESI DR2 + chronometers gives $\Delta\chi^2=-3.7117$ and $\Delta{\rm AIC}=-1.7117$ for constant-exponent R1. DES-Dovekie + DESI DR2 + chronometers gives $\Delta\chi^2=-4.2133$ and $\Delta{\rm AIC}=-2.2133$. In both cases BIC remains positive. The direct competitor audit is stricter: $w$CDM has a lower AIC than constant R1. The absorption branch R$\alpha$, however, gives $\Delta\chi^2=-4.5008$ and $\Delta{\rm AIC}=-2.5008$ while retaining \LCDM as both a zero-running and early-time limit. That is the strongest present background-level result. It still does not beat \LCDM by BIC. The current evidence is therefore suggestive but statistically inconclusive: R-Universe is best framed as an absorption program in which \LCDM is the frozen limit of a broader infrared-scale dynamics, not yet as a completed replacement for \LCDM.

The reason the program may still matter is sharper than the present $\chi^2$ improvement. The valuable claim is absorption: \LCDM is not discarded, but recovered as the frozen, early-time and zero-running limit of a relational infrared theory. If the same causal memory kernel that produces the R$\alpha$ background attractor also predicts a nontrivial relation among $\mu(k,z)$, $\eta(k,z)$ and $\Xi(z)$, then R-Universe would connect distance data, structure growth, gravitational slip and standard-siren propagation in a way that ordinary background-degenerate models do not. That is the possible textbook-changing result. It has not been demonstrated here; it has been formulated as the next decisive test.

\section{Computational and data provenance}

\subsection{Parameter vectors and minimization}

The fitted parameter vectors are
\[
\Theta_{\Lambda{\rm CDM}}=(H_0,\Omega_{\rm m0}),\qquad
\Theta_{\rm R1}=(H_0,\Omega_{\rm m0},\theta),\qquad
\Theta_{\rm R2}=(H_0,\Omega_{\rm m0},\theta,\nu).
\]
For each trial vector the solver first computes the positive physical branch of $E(z)$. \LCDM is evaluated analytically. R1 and R2 are evaluated by solving the implicit background equations on the redshift points required by the likelihood and by quadrature for distances. The solution branch is initialized at the \LCDM-connected root and rejected if the denominator in Eq.~\eqref{eq:eprime} crosses a singular value on the evaluated redshift domain.

The likelihood is not optimized independently per dataset. The objective minimized is always the full combined statistic
\[
\chi^2_{\rm tot}(\Theta)=
\chi^2_{\rm SN}(\Theta)+
\chi^2_{\rm BAO}(\Theta)+
\chi^2_{\rm CC}(\Theta).
\]
The fitted $\chi^2$ values are then decomposed by block after the best-fit vector is identified. This is why the component shifts in the main fit add to the quoted total shift.

\subsection{Pantheon+ calculation record}

The Pantheon+ calculation uses the following deterministic pipeline:
\begin{enumerate}
\item read \codepath{pantheon_plus.dat};
\item read \codepath{pantheon_plus_stat_sys.cov};
\item construct the boolean mask \codepath{is_calibrator == False} and $z_{\rm HD}>0.01$;
\item apply the same mask to the data vector and covariance rows/columns;
\item compute $D_C(z_{\rm HD})$ and $D_L=(1+z_{\rm hel})D_C$;
\item compute $\mu_{\rm th}=5\log_{10}(D_L/{\rm Mpc})+25$;
\item form residuals $r=\mu_{\rm obs}-\mu_{\rm th}$;
\item analytically marginalize the additive intercept using Eq.~\eqref{eq:snchi};
\item return $\chi^2_{\rm SN}$ to the global objective.
\end{enumerate}
The final retained Pantheon+ size is $1580$. In the main Pantheon+ + DESI DR2 + chronometer fit, the supernova block contributes $1388.620$ for \LCDM and $1386.116$ for R1 at their respective global best fits.

\subsection{DES-Dovekie calculation record}

The DES-Dovekie calculation is intentionally parallel to Pantheon+:
\begin{enumerate}
\item read \codepath{DES-Dovekie_HD.csv};
\item read the \codepath{STAT+SYS.npz} covariance matrix;
\item preserve the published Hubble-diagram ordering;
\item compute $D_L$ from the candidate cosmology;
\item analytically marginalize the additive intercept;
\item combine the resulting $\chi^2_{\rm SN}$ with DESI DR2 BAO and chronometers.
\end{enumerate}
The DES-Dovekie cross-check gives $\Delta\chi^2=-4.2133$ and $\Delta{\rm AIC}=-2.2133$ for R1 relative to \LCDM. Its agreement in sign with Pantheon+ is an important robustness check because it uses a different supernova pipeline and covariance product.

\subsection{DESI DR2 BAO calculation record}

The DESI DR2 BAO vector is evaluated as follows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item read \codepath{desi_gaussian_bao_ALL_GCcomb_mean.txt};
\item read \codepath{desi_gaussian_bao_ALL_GCcomb_cov.txt};
\item for each row identify whether the observable is $D_V/r_d$, $D_M/r_d$ or $D_H/r_d$;
\item compute $D_M$, $D_H$ and $D_V$ from the same $E(z)$ used in the supernova likelihood;
\item fix $r_d=147.09\,{\rm Mpc}$;
\item build the prediction vector in the published order;
\item evaluate the full-covariance quadratic form.
\end{enumerate}
For the main fit, the BAO block contributes $10.908$ for \LCDM, $9.638$ for R1 and $8.710$ for R2. The diagonal pulls in \codepath{tables/desi_dr2_bao_predictions.csv} are diagnostic only; the fit uses the full covariance matrix.

\subsection{Chronometer calculation record}

The chronometer block is a transparent diagonal likelihood:
\[
\chi^2_{\rm CC}=\sum_{i=1}^{31}\frac{[H_i-H_0E(z_i)]^2}{\sigma_{H,i}^2}.
\]
The numerical triplets are stored in \codepath{data/raw/chronometers/cosmic_chronometers.csv} and loaded by \codepath{code/r_universe_core.py}. The calculation does not yet include a full correlated chronometer systematic covariance. In the main fit, the chronometer block changes by only $+0.061$ between \LCDM and R1, so the R1 improvement is not driven by chronometer tuning.

\subsection{Derived predictions calculation record}

\codepath{code/derived_predictions.py} reads the main best-fit vectors from \codepath{code/extended_results.json}. It then computes:
\begin{itemize}
\item $q_0$ from $E'(z)$;
\item $j_0$ by differentiating $q(z)$ numerically and validating \LCDM with $j_0=0.999998$;
\item $w_R(0)$ and $w_R(1)$ using Eq.~\eqref{eq:wR};
\item the R1 future limit $H_\infty/H_0=(1-\Omega_{\rm m0})^{1/\theta}$;
\item the Sandage--Loeb velocity drift at $z=2$;
\item the normalized growth function $D(z)$ and growth rate $f(z)$ from Eq.~\eqref{eq:growth};
\item a minimal late-ISW source proxy from the redshift derivative of $D(z)(1+z)$.
\end{itemize}
The main R1 values are $\theta=1.61484$, $q_0=-0.49164$, $j_0=0.93419$, $w_R(0)=-0.93473$, $w_R(1)=-0.85249$, $H_\infty/H_0=0.80695$, and a maximum normalized $fD$ suppression of $3.42\%$ at $z\simeq0.33$.
