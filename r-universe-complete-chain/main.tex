\begin{abstract}
Flat \LCDM is an exceptionally successful empirical model, but it leaves the observed vacuum component as a rigid parameter rather than an explained infrared quantity. This paper presents a reproducible background-level late-time analysis of a minimal R-Universe infrared-scaling extension, supplemented by conditional subhorizon growth diagnostics. The physical premise is that the locally gravitating late-time vacuum sector may be an infrared effective component constrained by global or renormalization-group structure, rather than the raw additive zero-point energy. The minimal branch R1 is defined by
\[
E^2(z)-(1-\Omega_{\rm m0})E^{2-\theta}(z)-\Omega_{\rm m0}(1+z)^3=0,
\]
and contains flat \LCDM exactly and regularly at $\theta=2$. A second stress branch R2,
\[
E^2(z)-(1-\Omega_{\rm m0})(1+z)^\nu E^{2-\theta}(z)-\Omega_{\rm m0}(1+z)^3=0,
\]
tests whether additional shape freedom is statistically justified. The calculated chain includes the homogeneous background dynamics, supernova and BAO distances, profiled supernova intercepts, full-covariance DESI BAO likelihoods, cosmic chronometers, an alternative DES-Dovekie supernova-pipeline cross-check, numerical validation, profile likelihood in $\theta$, dataset robustness tests, a free-$r_d$ stress test, and a reference \LCDM CAMB baseline. For Pantheon+ full covariance + DESI DR2 BAO + cosmic chronometers, R1 gives $\Delta\chi^2=-3.7117$, $\Delta{\rm AIC}=-1.7117$ and $\Delta{\rm BIC}=+3.6809$ relative to \LCDM. The profiled likelihood gives $\theta=1.60$ with approximate $\Delta\chi^2\le1$ interval $[1.42,1.80]$ and $\Delta\chi^2\le4$ interval $[1.24,2.00]$; thus $\theta=2$ is only a suggestive $\simeq1.93\sigma$ late-time deviation, not a discovery. R1 is AIC-favored in the tested public late-time combinations, but not BIC-favored and not yet a replacement for a full perturbative Boltzmann/CMB implementation.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

The \LCDM model accurately describes the cosmic microwave background, baryon acoustic oscillations, Type Ia supernovae and large-scale structure \citep{Planck2018VI,DESI2024VI,Brout2022PantheonPlus}. Its success, however, does not explain why the vacuum sector should be a perfectly rigid cosmological constant. The cosmological constant problem remains a structural problem: a constant shift of the matter Lagrangian changes the stress tensor by a metric-proportional vacuum term and should gravitate in ordinary local Einstein equations \citep{Weinberg1989}.

R-Universe is tested here as a conservative and falsifiable late-time background extension. It does not assume that local general relativity fails in tested regimes. Instead, it asks whether the cosmological vacuum term entering the homogeneous infrared background can be represented by an effective infrared scaling law motivated by global constraints or renormalization-group flow. The model is deliberately constructed so that \LCDM is recovered exactly at the homogeneous background level. This makes the statistical question sharp: does the one-parameter deformation improve public data enough to pay for its extra parameter?

The paper is organized as an audit trail rather than a qualitative proposal. The calculated late-time chain is:
\[
\hbox{principle}\rightarrow
\hbox{background equation}\rightarrow
\hbox{observables}\rightarrow
\hbox{likelihoods}\rightarrow
\hbox{growth closure}\rightarrow
\hbox{statistics}\rightarrow
\hbox{predictions}.
\]
No fit number in this manuscript is quoted without a code path. The local and GitHub reproducibility package contains:
\begin{itemize}
\item \texttt{code/extended\_fit.py}: Pantheon+ full-covariance + DESI DR1/DR2 + chronometer fits;
\item \texttt{code/des\_dovekie\_fit.py}: DES-Dovekie STAT+SYS + DESI DR2 + chronometer cross-check;
\item \texttt{code/derived\_predictions.py}: cosmographic, growth and null-test predictions;
\item \texttt{code/make\_figures\_and\_tables.py}: figure and table generation;
\item \texttt{tables/all\_model\_fits.csv}: all fitted model summaries;
\item \texttt{tables/desi\_dr2\_bao\_predictions.csv}: BAO predictions and diagnostic pulls;
\item \texttt{data\_manifest/data\_sources.md}: public source URLs and local file mapping.
\end{itemize}

\section{Physical principle}

\subsection{Vacuum energy as an infrared observable}

The physical premise is not that arbitrary model flexibility should be added to \LCDM. It is that the observed vacuum component may be an infrared observable rather than the raw microscopic vacuum energy. Vacuum-energy sequestering frameworks show how global variables and constraints can remove sensitivity of local curvature to additive vacuum-energy shifts \citep{KaloperPadilla2014,KaloperPadilla2015}. R-Universe uses this as motivation, not as a completed derivation: the empirical model below is self-contained and can be falsified without assuming a unique microscopic sequestering implementation.

\subsection{Renormalization-group scaling}

In asymptotic-safety and functional-renormalization approaches, the effective gravitational action depends on a coarse-graining scale $k$ \citep{Reuter1998,ReuterSaueressig2019,BonannoSaueressig2017,Platania2020}. A dimensionless cosmological coupling $\lambda(k)=\Lambda(k)/k^2$ linearized around a fixed point satisfies schematically
\begin{equation}
k\frac{\dd}{\dd k}(\lambda-\lambda_*)=-\theta_{\rm RG}(\lambda-\lambda_*),
\end{equation}
so that
\begin{equation}
\Lambda(k)=\lambda_*k^2+Ck^{2-\theta_{\rm RG}}.
\end{equation}
Identifying the late-time infrared scale with $k\propto H$ motivates an effective component
\begin{equation}
\rho_R(H)=\rho_{R0}E^{2-\theta}.
\label{eq:rhoR}
\end{equation}
This step is an RG-motivated ansatz, not a derivation from a specified ultraviolet completion. The microscopic relation would be
\begin{equation}
\rho_\Lambda(k)=\frac{\Lambda(k)}{8\pi G(k)},
\end{equation}
so any running of $G(k)$ changes both the exponent and normalization. The fixed-point contribution $\lambda_*k^2$ maps to a term proportional to $H^2$ if $k=\xi H$ and can be partially degenerate with the left-hand side of the Friedmann equation or with a running Newton coupling. The present R1 model therefore absorbs these unknowns into the empirical normalization and exponent in Eq.~\eqref{eq:rhoR}. Alternative scale identifications such as $k^2\propto R$, $k\propto t^{-1}$, or $k^2\propto H^2+\alpha\dot H$ are not equivalent and are treated as theoretical systematics rather than completed calculations. The observational exponent $\theta$ is therefore an effective exponent $\theta_{\rm obs}$, not a direct measurement of a stability-matrix eigenvalue $\theta_{\rm RG}$. A future fundamental derivation must specify the truncation, regulator, gauge choice, running of $G$, Bianchi identity implementation, and energy exchange or non-exchange between matter and the R-sector.

\section{Background equations}

Assume a spatially flat late-time background and conserved pressureless matter,
\begin{equation}
\rho_m(z)=\rho_{m0}(1+z)^3.
\end{equation}
Using Eq.~\eqref{eq:rhoR}, the normalized Friedmann equation becomes
\begin{equation}
E^2(z)=\Omega_{\rm m0}(1+z)^3+(1-\Omega_{\rm m0})E^{2-\theta}(z).
\label{eq:r1}
\end{equation}
Equivalently,
\begin{equation}
F(E,z;\Omega_{\rm m0},\theta)=
E^2-(1-\Omega_{\rm m0})E^{2-\theta}
-\Omega_{\rm m0}(1+z)^3=0.
\end{equation}

\subsection{Exact embedding of \LCDM}

For $\theta=2$,
\begin{equation}
E^{2-\theta}=1,
\end{equation}
and Eq.~\eqref{eq:r1} becomes
\begin{equation}
E^2(z)=\Omega_{\rm m0}(1+z)^3+1-\Omega_{\rm m0},
\end{equation}
which is exactly flat \LCDM. Thus
\[
{\cal M}_{\Lambda{\rm CDM}}\subset{\cal M}_{\rm R1}
\]
at the background level. The limit is regular on the physical branch because
\begin{equation}
\frac{\partial F}{\partial E}
=2E-(1-\Omega_{\rm m0})(2-\theta)E^{1-\theta}
\rightarrow 2E>0
\end{equation}
at $\theta=2$ for $E>0$. The numerical solver therefore follows the positive branch connected to \LCDM. This is implemented in \texttt{code/extended\_fit.py}.

\subsection{R2 stress branch}

The R2 stress branch is
\begin{equation}
E^2-(1-\Omega_{\rm m0})(1+z)^\nu E^{2-\theta}
-\Omega_{\rm m0}(1+z)^3=0.
\label{eq:r2}
\end{equation}
It has one more parameter than R1 and two more than \LCDM. It is not treated as the preferred theory. It is a control experiment: if lower $\chi^2$ is bought only by extra flexibility, BIC should penalize it.

\section{Effective conservation and derived background quantities}

The effective R-sector density is
\begin{equation}
\rho_R(z)=\rho_{R0}E^{2-\theta}(z).
\end{equation}
If reconstructed as an effective fluid, its background equation of state is
\begin{equation}
w_R(z)=-1+\frac{2-\theta}{3}(1+z)\frac{E'(z)}{E(z)}.
\label{eq:wR}
\end{equation}
Implicit differentiation of R1 gives
\begin{equation}
E'(z)=
\frac{3\Omega_{\rm m0}(1+z)^2}
{2E-(1-\Omega_{\rm m0})(2-\theta)E^{1-\theta}}.
\label{eq:eprime}
\end{equation}
The main R1 fit gives $w_R(0)=-0.9347$ and $w_R(1)=-0.8525$, computed by \texttt{code/derived\_predictions.py}.

The deceleration and jerk are
\begin{align}
q(z)&=-1+(1+z)\frac{E'(z)}{E(z)},\\
j(z)&=q+2q^2+(1+z)\frac{\dd q}{\dd z}.
\end{align}
The code returns $j_0=0.999998$ for \LCDM, validating the numerical derivative, and $j_0=0.93419$ for the main R1 branch.

The future de Sitter limit for R1 is obtained by taking $z\rightarrow-1$:
\begin{equation}
\frac{H_\infty}{H_0}=(1-\Omega_{\rm m0})^{1/\theta}.
\end{equation}
For the main R1 fit, $H_\infty/H_0=0.80695$.

\section{Distances and likelihood observables}

The comoving distance is
\begin{equation}
D_C(z)=\frac{c}{H_0}\int_0^z\frac{\dd z'}{E(z')}.
\end{equation}
For a flat background,
\begin{align}
D_M(z)&=D_C(z),\\
D_H(z)&=\frac{c}{H_0E(z)},\\
D_V(z)&=\left[zD_M^2(z)D_H(z)\right]^{1/3}.
\end{align}
All BAO fits fix
\begin{equation}
r_d=147.09\ {\rm Mpc}.
\end{equation}
This is a late-time test. A future complete early-universe model must compute $r_d$.

For supernovae,
\begin{equation}
\mu_{\rm th}=5\log_{10}(D_L/{\rm Mpc})+25,
\end{equation}
with
\begin{equation}
D_L=(1+z_{\rm hel})D_C(z_{\rm HD})
\end{equation}
for Pantheon+. The absolute magnitude/offset is analytically marginalized:
\begin{equation}
\chi^2_{\rm SN}=r^TC^{-1}r-
\frac{(1^TC^{-1}r)^2}{1^TC^{-1}1}.
\label{eq:snchi}
\end{equation}

BAO and chronometer likelihoods are
\begin{align}
\chi^2_{\rm BAO}&=\Delta_{\rm BAO}^TC_{\rm BAO}^{-1}\Delta_{\rm BAO},\\
\chi^2_{\rm CC}&=\sum_i\left[\frac{H_i-H_0E(z_i)}{\sigma_{H,i}}\right]^2.
\label{eq:baocc}
\end{align}
The total statistic is
\begin{equation}
\chi^2_{\rm tot}=\chi^2_{\rm SN}+\chi^2_{\rm BAO}+\chi^2_{\rm CC}.
\end{equation}

\section{Conditional subhorizon growth forecast under a smooth R-sector GR closure}

A full perturbation theory requires an action or complete field equations. The present paper therefore states the conditional closure used for a late-time diagnostic calculation:
\begin{enumerate}
\item pressureless matter is conserved and minimally coupled;
\item the R-sector is smooth on the subhorizon scales used in the compressed growth check;
\item $G_{\rm eff}=G$ in the Poisson equation;
\item the background expansion is given by R1 or R2.
\end{enumerate}
Under this closure, matter growth satisfies
\begin{equation}
\frac{\dd^2D}{\dd\ln a^2}
\,+\,
\left[2+\frac{\dd\ln H}{\dd\ln a}\right]\frac{\dd D}{\dd\ln a}
-\frac{3}{2}\Omega_m(a)D=0,
\label{eq:growth}
\end{equation}
where
\begin{equation}
\Omega_m(a)=\frac{\Omega_{\rm m0}a^{-3}}{E^2(a)}.
\end{equation}
The solver starts at $a=10^{-3}$ with $D\propto a$ and normalizes to $D(0)=1$. The main R1 branch gives a maximum suppression of the normalized $fD$ proxy relative to \LCDM of $3.42\%$ at $z\simeq0.33$. This is not a prediction for $f\sigma_8$ until an amplitude parameter such as $\sigma_{8,0}$ or $A_s$ is specified.

For a CMB-relevant proxy, the minimal GR Weyl-potential scaling is
\begin{equation}
\Phi(z)\propto D(z)(1+z).
\end{equation}
The late ISW source is proportional to its time derivative. The code computes a redshift-derivative proxy and obtains, at $z=1$,
\[
{\cal I}_{\rm R1}=0.1011,\qquad
{\cal I}_{\Lambda{\rm CDM}}=0.0779.
\]
This proxy is kept as a diagnostic only. A real ISW observable requires $k$-dependent Weyl potentials, line-of-sight integration and survey window functions.

Pantheon+ is taken from the public PantheonPlusSH0ES release \citep{PantheonPlusDataRelease,Brout2022PantheonPlus}. The fit uses the public distance-modulus table and the full statistical-plus-systematic covariance:

\item covariance: \codepath{data/raw/pantheon_plus/pantheon_plus_stat_sys.cov};

\end{itemize}
The code removes Cepheid calibrators and applies $z_{\rm HD}>0.01$. The retained Pantheon+ distance-vector dimension is $N_{\rm SN}=1580$ entries. \rightarrow
\chi^2_{\rm SN}.

\subsection{Cosmic chronometers}

The chronometer block contains 31 published $H(z)$ points stored in \codepath{data/raw/chronometers/cosmic_chronometers.csv}. The file contains $z$, $H$, statistical uncertainty, reference, method, sample and notes columns. The current calculation uses the listed diagonal statistical uncertainties only. No covariance between chronometer points is assumed, so the implemented likelihood is the diagonal expression in Eq.~\eqref{eq:baocc}. This choice is transparent but incomplete; a correlated chronometer systematic covariance and SPS-model nuisance treatment are required for a final precision inference. The no-chronometer fit in Table~\ref{tab:robustness} is therefore treated as the primary robustness check against this limitation.

The computational path is
\[
\{z_i,H_i,\sigma_{H,i}\}
\rightarrow
H_{\rm th}(z_i)=H_0E(z_i)
\rightarrow
\chi^2_{\rm CC}
\rightarrow
\chi^2_{\rm tot}.
\]

\section{Computational reproducibility protocol}

\subsection{Software environment}

\subsection{Numerical validation}

The numerical validation file \texttt{code/numerical\_validation.json} is generated by \texttt{code/numerical\_validation.py}. It compares the production Newton solver against an independent bisection implementation on $0\le z\le3$ for the \LCDM limit, the main R1 solution and the main R2 solution. Within IEEE-754 double precision, the maximum scaled implicit-equation residuals are
\[
1.65\times10^{-16}\quad(\LCDM),\qquad
2.02\times10^{-16}\quad({\rm R1}),\qquad
1.82\times10^{-16}\quad({\rm R2}).
\]
The maximum relative Newton-versus-bisection differences are $2.22\times10^{-16}$ for \LCDM and $3.33\times10^{-16}$ for both R1 and R2. This does not mean the physical inference is known to 16 decimal places; it means the two implemented root finders return indistinguishable background branches over the tested domain. The \LCDM analytic branch validates the $\theta=2$ limit with maximum relative $E(z)$ error $2.22\times10^{-16}$ and gives $j_0=0.9999979$. A distance-integration check at $z=2$ gives $D_C=5256.50471\,{\rm Mpc}$ by trapezoidal integration and $5256.50468\,{\rm Mpc}$ by Simpson integration, a relative difference of $5.03\times10^{-9}$.

\subsection{Independent verification}

The independent verification is algorithmic rather than cosmetic. The production solver uses Newton iteration initialized on the positive \LCDM-connected branch and falls back to bisection if the scaled residual fails. The validation solver uses bisection directly and never calls the Newton update. Agreement at the $10^{-16}$ level on the production redshift domain demonstrates that the reported background values are not artifacts of a branch jump or Newton convergence failure.

\subsection{Statistical robustness status}

The current release contains deterministic best-fit and information-criterion comparisons plus a one-dimensional profile likelihood for $\theta$. It does not yet contain a completed MCMC chain, nested-sampling evidence, corner plot, posterior covariance matrix or prior-sensitivity scan. These objects must be generated before making a Nature-level claim that R-Universe decisively supersedes \LCDM. The manuscript therefore treats the AIC preference as an empirical signal, not as a final Bayesian model-selection result.

\subsection{Dataset robustness status}

The release contains the three main fitted combinations plus \texttt{code/robustness\_suite.py}, which computes SN-only, BAO-only, chronometer-only, no-chronometer, free-$r_d$, and leave-one-BAO-observable jackknife fits. Bootstrap resampling, alternative SN masks, external Hubble priors and full prior scans are not yet included.

\section{Model selection}

Model comparison uses
\begin{equation}
{\rm AIC}=\chi^2+2k,\qquad
{\rm BIC}=\chi^2+k\ln N,
\end{equation}
with $k=2$ for \LCDM, $k=3$ for R1 and $k=4$ for R2 \citep{Akaike1974,Schwarz1978}. Negative $\Delta$ values favor R-Universe relative to \LCDM.

The supernova intercept is common to all compared models. Including it in $k$ would shift all absolute AIC/BIC values equally within a fixed data combination and leave the reported model differences unchanged. The $N$ used in BIC is the dimension of the fitted data vector, e.g. $N=1580+13+31=1624$ for Pantheon+ + DESI DR2 BAO + chronometers. Because the supernova and BAO blocks are correlated, this is an operational BIC convention rather than a true count of statistically independent objects. The BIC values should therefore be read as a standard approximate penalty diagnostic; a nested-sampling evidence calculation is required for final Bayesian model comparison.

\section{Current limitations}

The present release is complete only as a background-level late-time reproducibility study with conditional growth diagnostics. The physical motivation is stated, the background equation is closed, the homogeneous \LCDM limit is exact, the public late-time likelihood observables are computed, data provenance is recorded, profile-likelihood and robustness stress tests are included, and the numerical results are generated by public code.

The following items are not completed calculations in this release:
\begin{enumerate}
\item the RG mapping from $\Lambda(k)$ and $G(k)$ to $\rho_R(H)$ is a motivated ansatz, not a derived theorem;
\item no covariant action or complete field equations for the R-sector are supplied;
\item $\theta$ is fitted phenomenologically and is not computed from a specified RG stability matrix;
\item no ghost or gradient-stability analysis is performed;
\item the growth equation assumes a smooth R-sector GR closure with $G_{\rm eff}=G$ and $\eta=1$;
\item no R1 CLASS/CAMB module, R1 CMB spectra, Planck likelihood or global CMB+BAO+SN+RSD/WL inference is completed;
\item no full MCMC, nested-sampling evidence, posterior covariance matrix or prior-sensitivity scan is yet included.
\end{enumerate}
These are not optional for a claim that R-Universe physically replaces or subsumes \LCDM. They are the next falsification gates.

\section{Conclusion}

R-Universe R1 is a closed, reproducible, one-parameter late-time background extension of flat \LCDM. It exactly contains \LCDM at $\theta=2$ at the homogeneous background level, computes all late-time distance observables used here, uses full covariance where available, includes an alternative DES-Dovekie supernova-pipeline cross-check, and gives conditional growth diagnostics under a stated smooth R-sector GR closure.

The empirical result is precise but modest. Pantheon+ full covariance + DESI DR2 + chronometers gives $\Delta\chi^2=-3.7117$ and $\Delta{\rm AIC}=-1.7117$. DES-Dovekie + DESI DR2 + chronometers gives $\Delta\chi^2=-4.2133$ and $\Delta{\rm AIC}=-2.2133$. In both cases BIC remains positive. The profile-likelihood interval includes $\theta=2$ at approximately the $\Delta\chi^2\le4$ level. The current evidence is therefore suggestive but statistically inconclusive: R1 is an AIC-supported, falsifiable late-time extension, not yet a definitive replacement for \LCDM.

\section{Computational and data provenance}

\subsection{Parameter vectors and minimization}

The fitted parameter vectors are
\[
\Theta_{\Lambda{\rm CDM}}=(H_0,\Omega_{\rm m0}),\qquad
\Theta_{\rm R1}=(H_0,\Omega_{\rm m0},\theta),\qquad
\Theta_{\rm R2}=(H_0,\Omega_{\rm m0},\theta,\nu).
\]
For each trial vector the solver first computes the positive physical branch of $E(z)$. \LCDM is evaluated analytically. R1 and R2 are evaluated by solving the implicit background equations on the redshift points required by the likelihood and by quadrature for distances. The solution branch is initialized at the \LCDM-connected root and rejected if the denominator in Eq.~\eqref{eq:eprime} crosses a singular value on the evaluated redshift domain.

The likelihood is not optimized independently per dataset. The objective minimized is always the full combined statistic
\[
\chi^2_{\rm tot}(\Theta)=
\chi^2_{\rm SN}(\Theta)+
\chi^2_{\rm BAO}(\Theta)+
\chi^2_{\rm CC}(\Theta).
\]
The fitted $\chi^2$ values are then decomposed by block after the best-fit vector is identified. This is why the component shifts in the main fit add to the quoted total shift.

\subsection{Pantheon+ calculation record}

The Pantheon+ calculation uses the following deterministic pipeline:
\begin{enumerate}
\item read \texttt{pantheon\_plus.dat};
\item read \texttt{pantheon\_plus\_stat\_sys.cov};
\item construct the boolean mask \texttt{is\_calibrator == False} and $z_{\rm HD}>0.01$;
\item apply the same mask to the data vector and covariance rows/columns;
\item compute $D_C(z_{\rm HD})$ and $D_L=(1+z_{\rm hel})D_C$;
\item compute $\mu_{\rm th}=5\log_{10}(D_L/{\rm Mpc})+25$;
\item form residuals $r=\mu_{\rm obs}-\mu_{\rm th}$;
\item analytically marginalize the additive intercept using Eq.~\eqref{eq:snchi};
\item return $\chi^2_{\rm SN}$ to the global objective.
\end{enumerate}
The final retained Pantheon+ size is $1580$. In the main Pantheon+ + DESI DR2 + chronometer fit, the supernova block contributes $1388.620$ for \LCDM and $1386.116$ for R1 at their respective global best fits.

\subsection{DES-Dovekie calculation record}

The DES-Dovekie calculation is intentionally parallel to Pantheon+:
\begin{enumerate}
\item read \texttt{DES-Dovekie\_HD.csv};
\item read the \texttt{STAT+SYS.npz} covariance matrix;
\item preserve the published Hubble-diagram ordering;
\item compute $D_L$ from the candidate cosmology;
\item analytically marginalize the additive intercept;
\item combine the resulting $\chi^2_{\rm SN}$ with DESI DR2 BAO and chronometers.
\end{enumerate}
The DES-Dovekie cross-check gives $\Delta\chi^2=-4.2133$ and $\Delta{\rm AIC}=-2.2133$ for R1 relative to \LCDM. Its agreement in sign with Pantheon+ is an important robustness check because it uses a different supernova pipeline and covariance product.

\subsection{DESI DR2 BAO calculation record}

The DESI DR2 BAO vector is evaluated as follows:
\begin{enumerate}
\item read \texttt{desi\_gaussian\_bao\_ALL\_GCcomb\_mean.txt};
\item read \texttt{desi\_gaussian\_bao\_ALL\_GCcomb\_cov.txt};
\item for each row identify whether the observable is $D_V/r_d$, $D_M/r_d$ or $D_H/r_d$;
\item compute $D_M$, $D_H$ and $D_V$ from the same $E(z)$ used in the supernova likelihood;
\item fix $r_d=147.09\,{\rm Mpc}$;
\item build the prediction vector in the published order;
\item evaluate the full-covariance quadratic form.
\end{enumerate}
For the main fit, the BAO block contributes $10.908$ for \LCDM, $9.638$ for R1 and $8.710$ for R2. The diagonal pulls in \texttt{tables/desi\_dr2\_bao\_predictions.csv} are diagnostic only; the fit uses the full covariance matrix.

\subsection{Chronometer calculation record}

The chronometer block is a transparent diagonal likelihood:
\[
\chi^2_{\rm CC}=\sum_{i=1}^{31}\frac{[H_i-H_0E(z_i)]^2}{\sigma_{H,i}^2}.
\]
The numerical triplets are stored in \texttt{data/raw/chronometers/cosmic\_chronometers.csv} and loaded by \texttt{code/r\_universe\_core.py}. The calculation does not yet include a full correlated chronometer systematic covariance. In the main fit, the chronometer block changes by only $+0.061$ between \LCDM and R1, so the R1 improvement is not driven by chronometer tuning.

\subsection{Derived predictions calculation record}

\texttt{code/derived\_predictions.py} reads the main best-fit vectors from \texttt{code/extended\_results.json}. It then computes:
\begin{itemize}
\item $q_0$ from $E'(z)$;
\item $j_0$ by differentiating $q(z)$ numerically and validating \LCDM with $j_0=0.999998$;
\item $w_R(0)$ and $w_R(1)$ using Eq.~\eqref{eq:wR};
\item the R1 future limit $H_\infty/H_0=(1-\Omega_{\rm m0})^{1/\theta}$;
\item the Sandage--Loeb velocity drift at $z=2$;
\item the normalized growth function $D(z)$ and growth rate $f(z)$ from Eq.~\eqref{eq:growth};
\item a minimal late-ISW source proxy from the redshift derivative of $D(z)(1+z)$.
\end{itemize}
The main R1 values are $\theta=1.61484$, $q_0=-0.49164$, $j_0=0.93419$, $w_R(0)=-0.93473$, $w_R(1)=-0.85249$, $H_\infty/H_0=0.80695$, and a maximum normalized $fD$ suppression of $3.42\%$ at $z\simeq0.33$.
