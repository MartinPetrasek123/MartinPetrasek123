\begin{abstract}
R-Universe is formulated here as a primary finite-window theory of geometric calibration. The starting point is not a dark component added to a pre-existing Friedmann equation, but a replacement of the pointwise gravitational response by a causal calibration law. The fundamental field is a scalar calibration variable $R_c$ obeying the retarded finite-window equation $(1-\ell^2\Box)R_c=-T/M_{\rm Pl}^2$. The locked theory uses one fixed window,
\begin{equation*}
W(R_c)=\frac{R_*}{R_c+R_*},
\end{equation*}
and the three functions
\begin{equation*}
F=1+\xi W(1-W),\qquad Z=Z_0W^2,
\qquad U=U_*W^2(1-W)^2.
\end{equation*}
These functions return the theory to ordinary local geometric response at both ends of the calibration range, $R_c\ll R_*$ and $R_c\gg R_*$, while activating modification only in the finite-window transition. The decisive calculation in this release is not a fit but the full multiplier stability gate. After integrating the multiplier term by parts, the scalar kinetic Hessian in the field velocities $(\dot R_c,\dot\lambda)$ is $K=\begin{pmatrix}Z&\ell^2\\\ell^2&0\end{pmatrix}$, with determinant $-\ell^4$. Therefore for every finite nonzero $\ell$ and positive $Z$ the two kinetic eigenvalues have opposite signs. The hard Lagrange-multiplier realization of the finite-window theory contains a ghost scalar degree of freedom before any data fitting. A reduced background branch is still integrated as a diagnostic, but it is not a physical solution of the full action because it omits $T_{\mu\nu}^{(\lambda)}$. The result is a useful failure: the finite-window principle survives as a target, while this specific multiplier action does not.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} R-Universe; finite-window calibration; causal gravity; scalar--tensor cosmology; modified Friedmann equations; cosmic acceleration; perturbations.

\section{Core Principle}

R-Universe replaces instantaneous pointwise gravitational response by causal finite-window calibration. The primitive relation is
\begin{equation}
G_{\mu\nu}+\mathcal C_{\mu\nu}
=\frac{1}{M_{\rm Pl}^2}\mathcal W_\ell T_{\mu\nu},
\label{eq:core-relation}
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal W_\ell$ is a retarded finite-window operator and $\mathcal C_{\mu\nu}$ is the covariant correction required by the Bianchi identity. The defining conservation condition is
\begin{equation}
\nabla^\mu\left(\mathcal W_\ell T_{\mu\nu}-M_{\rm Pl}^2\mathcal C_{\mu\nu}\right)=0.
\label{eq:conservation-core}
\end{equation}
This is the central replacement: geometry responds to a causally calibrated source, not to an infinitely sharp local sample of stress energy.

The finite-window operator is fixed as the retarded resolvent
\begin{equation}
\mathcal W_\ell=(1-\ell^2\Box_{\rm ret})^{-1}.
\label{eq:retarded-window}
\end{equation}
The sign convention is chosen so that the local auxiliary-field representation is hyperbolic and retarded.

\section{Locked Action}

The local variational form uses a calibration scalar $R_c$ and a Lagrange multiplier $\lambda$:
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
S_R=\int\dd^4x\sqrt{-g}\left[
\frac{M_{\rm Pl}^2}{2}F(R_c)\mathcal R
-\frac{Z(R_c)}{2}\nabla_\mu R_c\nabla^\mu R_c
-U(R_c)
+\lambda\left((1-\ell^2\Box)R_c+\frac{T}{M_{\rm Pl}^2}\right)
\right]+S_m.
}
\label{eq:locked-action}
\end{equation}
Here $\mathcal R$ is the Ricci scalar. Integrating the multiplier term by parts gives the dynamically transparent form
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
S_R=\int\dd^4x\sqrt{-g}\left[
\frac{M_{\rm Pl}^2}{2}F\mathcal R
-\frac{Z}{2}(\nabla R_c)^2-U
+\lambda R_c
-\ell^2\nabla_\mu\lambda\nabla^\mu R_c
+\lambda\frac{T}{M_{\rm Pl}^2}
\right]+S_m .
}
\label{eq:ibp-action}
\end{equation}
The calibration source is
\begin{equation}
\mathcal I[g,T]=-\frac{T}{M_{\rm Pl}^2}.
\label{eq:cal-source}
\end{equation}
Variation with respect to $\lambda$ gives the finite-window equation
\begin{equation}
\boxed{(1-\ell^2\Box)R_c=-\frac{T}{M_{\rm Pl}^2}.}
\label{eq:calibration-equation}
\end{equation}
Variation with respect to $R_c$ gives the independent multiplier equation
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
\ell^2\Box\lambda+\lambda
+\frac{M_{\rm Pl}^2}{2}F_{,R}\mathcal R
+\frac12 Z_{,R}(\nabla R_c)^2
+Z\Box R_c
-U_{,R}=0.
}
\label{eq:Rvariation}
\end{equation}
Thus the full background system contains at least $\{a,H,R_c,\dot R_c,\lambda,\dot\lambda,\rho_m,\rho_r\}$. The multiplier cannot be used to impose Eq.~\eqref{eq:calibration-equation} and then removed from the stress tensor.

The window and locked functions are
\begin{align}
W(R_c)&=\frac{R_*}{R_c+R_*},\label{eq:Wlocked}\\
F(R_c)&=1+\xi W(1-W),\label{eq:Flocked}\\
Z(R_c)&=Z_0W^2,\label{eq:Zlocked}\\
U(R_c)&=U_*W^2(1-W)^2.\label{eq:Ulocked}
\end{align}
No additional redshift function, expansion history, or constant vacuum term is inserted. The modification lives only in the transition window. In the two limits,
\begin{equation}
R_c\ll R_*:\quad W\to1,\quad F\to1,\quad U\to0,
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
R_c\gg R_*:\quad W\to0,\quad F\to1,\quad U\to0.
\end{equation}
Thus dense local systems and the high-density early branch both return to the uncalibrated geometric response, while the finite-window regime $R_c\sim R_*$ carries the new dynamics.

\section{Field Equations}

Metric variation gives
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
M_{\rm Pl}^2F G_{\mu\nu}
=T_{\mu\nu}^{(m)}+T_{\mu\nu}^{(R)}
+M_{\rm Pl}^2\left(\nabla_\mu\nabla_\nu F-g_{\mu\nu}\Box F\right)
+T_{\mu\nu}^{(\lambda)}.
}
\label{eq:metric-equation}
\end{equation}
The calibration stress tensor is
\begin{equation}
T_{\mu\nu}^{(R)}=Z\nabla_\mu R_c\nabla_\nu R_c
-g_{\mu\nu}\left[\frac{Z}{2}(\nabla R_c)^2+U\right].
\label{eq:TR}
\end{equation}
This is not a dark-fluid addition. The same field changes the effective Planck mass, the background expansion, the perturbative response, and the relation between matter and geometry.

\section{Background Equations}

For a flat FLRW metric and a homogeneous calibration field, the first equation is
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
3M_{\rm Pl}^2F H^2
=\rho_m+\rho_r+\frac12Z\dot R_c^2+U
-3M_{\rm Pl}^2H\dot F+\rho_\lambda.
}
\label{eq:friedmann1locked}
\end{equation}
The second equation is
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
-2M_{\rm Pl}^2F\dot H
=\rho_m+\frac43\rho_r+Z\dot R_c^2
+M_{\rm Pl}^2(\ddot F-H\dot F)+\rho_\lambda+p_\lambda.
}
\label{eq:friedmann2locked}
\end{equation}
For dust plus radiation, $T=-\rho_m$, and Eq.~\eqref{eq:calibration-equation} becomes
\begin{equation}
\boxed{\ddot R_c+3H\dot R_c+\ell^{-2}R_c=\ell^{-2}\rho_m/M_{\rm Pl}^2.}
\label{eq:Rflrw}
\end{equation}
The reduced numerical background shown below sets $\rho_\lambda=p_\lambda=0$ only as a diagnostic of the non-multiplier subsystem. It is not a physical solution of Eq.~\eqref{eq:ibp-action}. The full theory must keep $T_{\mu\nu}^{(\lambda)}$ and evolve $\lambda$ with Eq.~\eqref{eq:Rvariation}.

The R-critical density variables are defined by
\begin{align}
\Omega_m^R&=\frac{\rho_m}{3M_{\rm Pl}^2F H^2},\label{eq:OmmR}\\
\Omega_r^R&=\frac{\rho_r}{3M_{\rm Pl}^2F H^2},\label{eq:OmrR}\\
\Omega_R&=\frac{\frac12Z\dot R_c^2+U-3M_{\rm Pl}^2H\dot F+\rho_\lambda}{3M_{\rm Pl}^2F H^2}.
\label{eq:OmcalR}
\end{align}
Then the first equation gives the exact closure relation
\begin{equation}
\boxed{\Omega_m^R+\Omega_r^R+\Omega_R=1.}
\label{eq:Rclosure}
\end{equation}


\section{Full Multiplier Kinetic Gate}

The decisive scalar-sector test can be performed before any observational fit. From Eq.~\eqref{eq:ibp-action}, the homogeneous kinetic terms for the two scalar variables are
\begin{equation}
L_{\rm kin}=a^3\left[\frac12 Z(R_c)\dot R_c^2+\ell^2\dot\lambda\dot R_c\right].
\label{eq:Lkin}
\end{equation}
Therefore the velocity Hessian in the basis $(\dot R_c,\dot\lambda)$ is
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
K=
\begin{pmatrix}
Z(R_c)&\ell^2\\
\ell^2&0
\end{pmatrix}.
}
\label{eq:Kmatrix}
\end{equation}
Its determinant is
\begin{equation}
\boxed{\det K=-\ell^4.}
\label{eq:detK}
\end{equation}
For every finite nonzero $\ell$, $\det K<0$. Hence the two eigenvalues have opposite signs,
\begin{equation}
K_\pm=\frac12\left[Z\pm\sqrt{Z^2+4\ell^4}\right],
\label{eq:Keigs}
\end{equation}
with $K_+>0$ and $K_-<0$ for $Z>0$. This is a background-independent ghost in the hard Lagrange-multiplier construction. The script \codepath{code/full_lambda_stability_gate.py} evaluates Eq.~\eqref{eq:Keigs} on the integrated branch and finds
\begin{equation}
\min K_+=0.490000,
\qquad
\max K_-=-0.465637,
\qquad
\min K_-=-0.490000.
\label{eq:ghost-result}
\end{equation}
The verdict is therefore immediate: this locked multiplier action is not viable in its present form. No background fit can repair a propagating ghost.

\section{Perturbative Sector}

In Newtonian gauge,
\begin{equation}
\dd s^2=-(1+2\Psi)\dd t^2+a^2(1-2\Phi)\dd\mathbf x^2.
\end{equation}
Linearization of the same locked functions gives
\begin{align}
-\frac{k^2}{a^2}\Psi&=4\pi G\,\mu(k,a)\rho_m\Delta_m,\label{eq:mu}\\
\Phi&=\eta(k,a)\Psi,\label{eq:eta}\\
-\frac{k^2}{a^2}(\Phi+\Psi)&=8\pi G\,\Sigma(k,a)\rho_m\Delta_m.
\label{eq:sigma}
\end{align}
In the quasistatic scalar--tensor limit the expected form is
\begin{equation}
\mu(k,a)=\frac1F\left[1+\frac{2F_R^2}{ZF+3F_R^2}
\frac{k^2/a^2}{k^2/a^2+m_R^2}\right],
\label{eq:mu-form}
\end{equation}
with
\begin{equation}
m_R^2=\frac{U_{RR}^{\rm eff}}{Z_{\rm eff}}.
\end{equation}
The slip is
\begin{equation}
\eta(k,a)=\frac{1-\epsilon(k,a)}{1+\epsilon(k,a)},
\qquad
\epsilon(k,a)=\frac{F_R^2}{ZF+2F_R^2}
\frac{k^2/a^2}{k^2/a^2+m_R^2}.
\label{eq:eta-form}
\end{equation}
Thus $\mu$, $\eta$ and $\Sigma$ are not independent functions. They are consequences of $F$, $Z$ and $U$.

The tensor quadratic action is
\begin{equation}
S_T^{(2)}=\frac18\int\dd t\dd^3x\,a^3M_{\rm Pl}^2F
\left[\dot h_{ij}^2-\frac{(\nabla h_{ij})^2}{a^2}\right].
\label{eq:tensorlocked}
\end{equation}
Therefore
\begin{equation}
\boxed{Q_T=M_{\rm Pl}^2F>0,\qquad c_T^2=1.}
\label{eq:tensor-gates}
\end{equation}
The scalar quadratic action must have
\begin{equation}
S_s^{(2)}=\int\dd t\dd^3x\,a^3Q_s
\left[\dot\zeta^2-c_s^2\frac{(\nabla\zeta)^2}{a^2}\right],
\end{equation}
with
\begin{equation}
\boxed{Q_s>0,\qquad c_s^2>0.}
\label{eq:scalar-stability}
\end{equation}
The current calculation verifies $F>0$, $Z>0$ and $c_T^2=1$ on the integrated branch. It does not yet compute the full scalar quadratic action.

\section{Completion Gates}

This version defines the locked multiplier theory and performs the first decisive internal test. Since the hard multiplier kinetic matrix has a negative eigenvalue, the required next steps are not data fitting but repair or replacement of the multiplier sector:
\begin{enumerate}
\item keep the locked functions in Eqs.~\eqref{eq:Wlocked}--\eqref{eq:Ulocked};
\item replace the hard multiplier by a healthy auxiliary field, degenerate constraint, or non-propagating construction;
\item only then derive the full scalar quadratic action and compute $Q_s$ and $c_s^2$;
\item compute $\mu$, $\eta$ and $\Sigma$ from Eqs.~\eqref{eq:mu-form}--\eqref{eq:eta-form};
\item implement the same equations in a Boltzmann solver;
\item search parameter space for a branch that keeps a nonzero R-critical matter fraction while maintaining acceleration and $F>0$.
\end{enumerate}
No function may be changed after these gates are declared. The present hard-multiplier action has already failed the kinetic gate; the finite-window idea must move to a ghost-free realization.
