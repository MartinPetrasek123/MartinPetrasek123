\begin{abstract}
R-Universe is tested here through a deliberately narrow finite-window localization: a scalar calibration variable $R_c$ is sourced by the trace $T$ of the matter stress tensor through $(1-\ell^2\Box)R_c=-T/M_{\rm Pl}^2$. This is not yet a tensor calibration of the full source $T_{\mu\nu}$. It is the first hard local test of whether an exact finite-window constraint can be imposed by a Lagrange multiplier without introducing an unphysical scalar mode. The locked prototype uses one fixed window,
\begin{equation*}
W(R_c)=\frac{R_*}{R_c+R_*},
\end{equation*}
and the three functions
\begin{equation*}
F=1+\xi W(1-W),\qquad Z=Z_0W^2,
\qquad U=U_*W^2(1-W)^2.
\end{equation*}
These functions make $F\to1$ and $U\to0$ at both ends of the calibration range while activating modification only in the finite-window transition; this by itself is not a proof of a complete general-relativistic limit because the auxiliary kinetic and trace-coupling sectors must also decouple. The decisive calculation in this release is not a fit but the multiplier stability gate. After integrating the multiplier term by parts, the scalar kinetic Hessian in the field velocities $(\dot R_c,\dot\lambda)$ is $K=\begin{pmatrix}Z&\ell^2\\\ell^2&0\end{pmatrix}$, with determinant $-\ell^4$. The non-minimal $F(R_c)\mathcal R$ term adds a positive contribution to the $R_cR_c$ entry after the metric kinetic mixing is diagonalized, but the determinant remains negative, $\det K_E=-\ell^4/F^2$, for $F>0$. Therefore the hard Lagrange-multiplier localization has an indefinite scalar kinetic signature before any data fitting. A reduced background branch is integrated only as a diagnostic; it is not a physical solution of the full action because it omits $T_{\mu\nu}^{(\lambda)}$ and does not close the matter variation induced by $\lambda T$. The result is a useful no-go statement for this localization, not a disproof of the finite-window principle.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} R-Universe; finite-window calibration; causal gravity; scalar--tensor cosmology; modified Friedmann equations; cosmic acceleration; perturbations.

\section{Core Principle}

The broad R-Universe target is to replace instantaneous pointwise gravitational response by finite-window calibration. A tensor version of that target would have the schematic form
\begin{equation}
G_{\mu\nu}+\mathcal C_{\mu\nu}
=\frac{1}{M_{\rm Pl}^2}\mathcal W_\ell T_{\mu\nu},
\label{eq:core-relation}
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal W_\ell$ is a finite-window operator and $\mathcal C_{\mu\nu}$ is the covariant correction required by the Bianchi identity. The defining conservation condition would be
\begin{equation}
\nabla^\mu\left(\mathcal W_\ell T_{\mu\nu}-M_{\rm Pl}^2\mathcal C_{\mu\nu}\right)=0.
\label{eq:conservation-core}
\end{equation}
The local action tested in this paper does not realize Eq.~\eqref{eq:core-relation}. It realizes only a scalar trace localization,
\begin{equation}
(1-\ell^2\Box)R_c=-\frac{T}{M_{\rm Pl}^2}.
\label{eq:trace-only}
\end{equation}
This distinction is essential. Radiation has $T=0$ but $T_{\mu\nu}\neq0$, so a trace-sourced calibration field does not calibrate the full gravitational source. A true tensor finite-window theory would require an auxiliary tensor or constrained tensor operator, for example
\begin{equation}
(1-\ell^2\Box)X_{\mu\nu}=\frac{T_{\mu\nu}}{M_{\rm Pl}^2},
\qquad
\nabla^\mu X_{\mu\nu}=0,
\label{eq:tensor-target}
\end{equation}
or an equivalent covariant construction. The present manuscript is therefore a no-go test of the trace-scalar Lagrange-multiplier localization, not the completed tensor theory.

The desired causal branch is the retarded resolvent
\begin{equation}
\mathcal W_\ell=(1-\ell^2\Box_{\rm ret})^{-1}.
\label{eq:retarded-window}
\end{equation}
A single-copy local variational action does not by itself select the retarded Green function; its Euler--Lagrange equations allow homogeneous solutions. The retarded branch must be imposed by boundary conditions or by an in-in effective action. This is the standard subtlety in local representations of nonlocal operators and is one reason the stability gate below is treated as a test of a localization, not as a finished causal theory \citep{MaggioreMancarella2014,Woodard2014,FoffaMaggiore2014}.

\section{Locked Action}

The local variational form uses a calibration scalar $R_c$ and a Lagrange multiplier $\lambda$:
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
S_R=\int\dd^4x\sqrt{-g}\left[
\frac{M_{\rm Pl}^2}{2}F(R_c)\mathcal R
-\frac{Z(R_c)}{2}\nabla_\mu R_c\nabla^\mu R_c
-U(R_c)
+\lambda\left((1-\ell^2\Box)R_c+\frac{T}{M_{\rm Pl}^2}\right)
\right]+S_m.
}
\label{eq:locked-action}
\end{equation}
Here $\mathcal R$ is the Ricci scalar. Integrating the multiplier term by parts gives the dynamically transparent form
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
S_R=\int\dd^4x\sqrt{-g}\left[
\frac{M_{\rm Pl}^2}{2}F\mathcal R
-\frac{Z}{2}(\nabla R_c)^2-U
+\lambda R_c
-\ell^2\nabla_\mu\lambda\nabla^\mu R_c
+\lambda\frac{T}{M_{\rm Pl}^2}
\right]+S_m .
}
\label{eq:ibp-action}
\end{equation}
The calibration source is
\begin{equation}
\mathcal I[g,T]=-\frac{T}{M_{\rm Pl}^2}.
\label{eq:cal-source}
\end{equation}
Variation with respect to $\lambda$ gives the finite-window equation
\begin{equation}
\boxed{(1-\ell^2\Box)R_c=-\frac{T}{M_{\rm Pl}^2}.}
\label{eq:calibration-equation}
\end{equation}
Variation with respect to $R_c$ gives the independent multiplier equation
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
\ell^2\Box\lambda+\lambda
+\frac{M_{\rm Pl}^2}{2}F_{,R}\mathcal R
+\frac12 Z_{,R}(\nabla R_c)^2
+Z\Box R_c
-U_{,R}=0.
}
\label{eq:Rvariation}
\end{equation}
Thus the full background system contains at least $\{a,H,R_c,\dot R_c,\lambda,\dot\lambda,\rho_m,\rho_r\}$. The multiplier cannot be used to impose Eq.~\eqref{eq:calibration-equation} and then removed from the stress tensor.

The dimensions are fixed by Eq.~\eqref{eq:calibration-equation}. In units with $c=\hbar=1$,
\begin{equation}
[R_c]=[R_*]=M^2,\qquad [\ell]=M^{-1},\qquad [U_*]=M^4,\qquad [Z_0]=M^{-2},\qquad [\lambda]=M^2.
\label{eq:dimensions}
\end{equation}
The numerical scan below uses a dimensionless reporting scale $H_*$ with
\begin{equation}
R_*=0.2H_*^2,\qquad U_*=1.5M_{\rm Pl}^2H_*^2,\qquad Z_0=0.05H_*^{-2},\qquad \ell=0.7H_*^{-1}.
\label{eq:units}
\end{equation}
The parameter labelled $\rho_{m0}$ in the code is therefore a dimensionless branch normalization, not a measured present-day matter density.

The window and locked functions are
\begin{align}
W(R_c)&=\frac{R_*}{R_c+R_*},\label{eq:Wlocked}\\
F(R_c)&=1+\xi W(1-W),\label{eq:Flocked}\\
Z(R_c)&=Z_0W^2,\label{eq:Zlocked}\\
U(R_c)&=U_*W^2(1-W)^2.\label{eq:Ulocked}
\end{align}
No additional redshift function, expansion history, or constant vacuum term is inserted. The modification lives only in the transition window. In the two limits,
\begin{equation}
R_c\ll R_*:\quad W\to1,\quad F\to1,\quad U\to0,
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
R_c\gg R_*:\quad W\to0,\quad F\to1,\quad U\to0.
\end{equation}
These limits prove only $F\to1$ and $U\to0$. They do not prove full recovery of ordinary local gravity, because $Z(\nabla R_c)^2$, $-\ell^2\nabla_\mu\lambda\nabla^\mu R_c$ and $\lambda T/M_{\rm Pl}^2$ can still contribute. A complete local-gravity limit requires all auxiliary sectors to decouple.

The rational window also has a pole at
\begin{equation}
R_c=-R_*.
\label{eq:window-pole}
\end{equation}
The homogeneous dust branch used here remains in $R_c>0$, but this is not a proof of an invariant field domain under perturbations or in vacuum regions. A successor theory must either prove $R_c>-R_*$ for admissible data or replace Eq.~\eqref{eq:Wlocked} by a regular window such as $W=(1+(R_c/R_*)^2)^{-1}$.

\section{Field Equations}

Metric variation gives
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
M_{\rm Pl}^2F G_{\mu\nu}
=T_{\mu\nu}^{(m)}+T_{\mu\nu}^{(R)}
+M_{\rm Pl}^2\left(\nabla_\mu\nabla_\nu F-g_{\mu\nu}\Box F\right)
+T_{\mu\nu}^{(\lambda)}.
}
\label{eq:metric-equation}
\end{equation}
The calibration stress tensor is
\begin{equation}
T_{\mu\nu}^{(R)}=Z\nabla_\mu R_c\nabla_\nu R_c
-g_{\mu\nu}\left[\frac{Z}{2}(\nabla R_c)^2+U\right].
\label{eq:TR}
\end{equation}
The multiplier stress tensor is not optional. From the derivative and algebraic pieces in Eq.~\eqref{eq:ibp-action}, it contains terms of the form
\begin{equation}
T_{\mu\nu}^{(\lambda,{\rm der})}
=\ell^2\left[
\nabla_\mu\lambda\nabla_\nu R_c+\nabla_\nu\lambda\nabla_\mu R_c
-g_{\mu\nu}\nabla_\alpha\lambda\nabla^\alpha R_c
\right],
\label{eq:Tlambda-der}
\end{equation}
and an algebraic part from $\lambda R_c$. The trace coupling requires still more care because
\begin{equation}
\delta T=T_{\mu\nu}\delta g^{\mu\nu}
+g^{\alpha\beta}\frac{\delta T_{\alpha\beta}}{\delta g^{\mu\nu}}\delta g^{\mu\nu}
\equiv \left(T_{\mu\nu}+\Theta_{\mu\nu}\right)\delta g^{\mu\nu}.
\label{eq:Theta}
\end{equation}
Therefore the term $\lambda T/M_{\rm Pl}^2$ generally contributes a matter-Lagrangian-dependent tensor proportional to $T_{\mu\nu}+\Theta_{\mu\nu}$ and also modifies the matter equations. Standard continuity equations for matter and radiation cannot be assumed until this sector is specified \citep{Harko2011,NojiriOdintsov2011}. This is another reason the reduced branch below is diagnostic only.

The tensor target in Eq.~\eqref{eq:tensor-target}, the trace-scalar localization in Eq.~\eqref{eq:trace-only}, and the degeneracy requirement discussed below sit in the same technical territory as causal nonlocal gravity, scalar--tensor stability and degenerate higher-order scalar--tensor theories \citep{MaggioreMancarella2014,FoffaMaggiore2014,Woodard2014,Langlois2016,DHOST2018}. The tensor speed condition is also constrained by multimessenger bounds \citep{CreminelliVernizzi2017,EzquiagaZumalacarregui2017}.

\section{Background Equations}

For a flat FLRW metric and a homogeneous calibration field, the first equation is
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
3M_{\rm Pl}^2F H^2
=\rho_m+\rho_r+\frac12Z\dot R_c^2+U
-3M_{\rm Pl}^2H\dot F+\rho_\lambda.
}
\label{eq:friedmann1locked}
\end{equation}
The second equation is
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
-2M_{\rm Pl}^2F\dot H
=\rho_m+\frac43\rho_r+Z\dot R_c^2
+M_{\rm Pl}^2(\ddot F-H\dot F)+\rho_\lambda+p_\lambda.
}
\label{eq:friedmann2locked}
\end{equation}
For dust plus radiation, $T=-\rho_m$, and Eq.~\eqref{eq:calibration-equation} becomes
\begin{equation}
\boxed{\ddot R_c+3H\dot R_c+\ell^{-2}R_c=\ell^{-2}\rho_m/M_{\rm Pl}^2.}
\label{eq:Rflrw}
\end{equation}
The reduced numerical background shown below sets $\rho_\lambda=p_\lambda=0$ only as a diagnostic of the non-multiplier subsystem. It is not a physical solution of Eq.~\eqref{eq:ibp-action}. The full theory must keep $T_{\mu\nu}^{(\lambda)}$ and evolve $\lambda$ with Eq.~\eqref{eq:Rvariation}.

The explicit homogeneous derivative contribution would include
\begin{equation}
\rho_{\lambda,{\rm der}}=\ell^2\dot\lambda\dot R_c,
\qquad
p_{\lambda,{\rm der}}=\ell^2\dot\lambda\dot R_c,
\label{eq:lambdader-rhop}
\end{equation}
together with algebraic and matter-variation terms from $\lambda R_c+\lambda T/M_{\rm Pl}^2$. Since these terms are not included in \codepath{code/locked_r_universe.py}, that script does not solve the complete action.

The R-critical density variables are defined by
\begin{align}
\Omega_m^R&=\frac{\rho_m}{3M_{\rm Pl}^2F H^2},\label{eq:OmmR}\\
\Omega_r^R&=\frac{\rho_r}{3M_{\rm Pl}^2F H^2},\label{eq:OmrR}\\
\Omega_R&=\frac{\frac12Z\dot R_c^2+U-3M_{\rm Pl}^2H\dot F+\rho_\lambda}{3M_{\rm Pl}^2F H^2}.
\label{eq:OmcalR}
\end{align}
Then the first equation gives the exact closure relation
\begin{equation}
\boxed{\Omega_m^R+\Omega_r^R+\Omega_R=1.}
\label{eq:Rclosure}
\end{equation}


\section{Full Multiplier Kinetic Gate}

The decisive scalar-sector test can be performed before any observational fit. From Eq.~\eqref{eq:ibp-action}, the homogeneous kinetic terms for the two scalar variables are
\begin{equation}
L_{\rm kin}=a^3\left[\frac12 Z(R_c)\dot R_c^2+\ell^2\dot\lambda\dot R_c\right].
\label{eq:Lkin}
\end{equation}
Therefore the velocity Hessian in the basis $(\dot R_c,\dot\lambda)$ is
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
K=
\begin{pmatrix}
Z(R_c)&\ell^2\\
\ell^2&0
\end{pmatrix}.
}
\label{eq:Kmatrix}
\end{equation}
Its determinant is
\begin{equation}
\boxed{\det K=-\ell^4.}
\label{eq:detK}
\end{equation}
For every finite nonzero $\ell$, $\det K<0$. Hence the two eigenvalues have opposite signs,
\begin{equation}
K_\pm=\frac12\left[Z\pm\sqrt{Z^2+4\ell^4}\right],
\label{eq:Keigs}
\end{equation}
with $K_+>0$ and $K_-<0$ for $Z>0$. This is a background-independent ghost in the hard Lagrange-multiplier construction. The script \codepath{code/full_lambda_stability_gate.py} evaluates Eq.~\eqref{eq:Keigs} on the integrated branch and finds
\begin{equation}
\min K_+=0.490000,
\qquad
\max K_-=-0.465637,
\qquad
\min K_-=-0.490000.
\label{eq:ghost-result}
\end{equation}
Because $\det K\neq0$, the pair $(R_c,\lambda)$ is not a degenerate constraint system at the homogeneous kinetic level. The variable called a multiplier has become part of a propagating two-scalar sector, with one negative kinetic direction.

The non-minimal term $F(R_c)\mathcal R$ cannot remove this conclusion by adding a positive contribution only to the $R_cR_c$ entry. In an Einstein-frame estimate, the scalar entry becomes
\begin{equation}
Z_E=\frac{Z}{F}+\frac32\left(\frac{F_{,R}}{F}\right)^2,
\label{eq:ZE}
\end{equation}
and the off-diagonal term scales as $\ell^2/F$. Thus
\begin{equation}
K_E\simeq
\begin{pmatrix}
Z_E&\ell^2/F\\
\ell^2/F&0
\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
\boxed{\det K_E=-\frac{\ell^4}{F^2}<0}
\label{eq:KE}
\end{equation}
for $F>0$. Numerically, \codepath{code/full_lambda_stability_gate.py} finds
\begin{equation}
\min K_+^E=0.454615,
\qquad
\max K_-^E=-2.59309\times10^{-4},
\qquad
\min K_-^E=-0.489990.
\label{eq:ghost-result-E}
\end{equation}
The homogeneous Hessian therefore already has indefinite signature. The final theorem for the full metric-scalar system would require an ADM analysis and elimination of lapse and shift, but the present gate is strong enough to rule out parameter tuning as a cure. A background fit cannot repair a non-degenerate negative kinetic mode \citep{Langlois2016,DHOST2018}.

\section{Perturbative Status}

In Newtonian gauge,
\begin{equation}
\dd s^2=-(1+2\Psi)\dd t^2+a^2(1-2\Phi)\dd\mathbf x^2.
\end{equation}
For a healthy one-scalar successor theory, linear observables would be organized by
\begin{align}
-\frac{k^2}{a^2}\Psi&=4\pi G\,\mu(k,a)\rho_m\Delta_m,\label{eq:mu}\\
\Phi&=\eta(k,a)\Psi,\label{eq:eta}\\
-\frac{k^2}{a^2}(\Phi+\Psi)&=8\pi G\,\Sigma(k,a)\rho_m\Delta_m.
\label{eq:sigma}
\end{align}
In the quasistatic limit of a simpler healthy scalar--tensor theory, the expected form would be
\begin{equation}
\mu(k,a)=\frac1F\left[1+\frac{2F_R^2}{ZF+3F_R^2}
\frac{k^2/a^2}{k^2/a^2+m_R^2}\right],
\label{eq:mu-form}
\end{equation}
with
\begin{equation}
m_R^2=\frac{U_{RR}^{\rm eff}}{Z_{\rm eff}}.
\end{equation}
The slip is
\begin{equation}
\eta(k,a)=\frac{1-\epsilon(k,a)}{1+\epsilon(k,a)},
\qquad
\epsilon(k,a)=\frac{F_R^2}{ZF+2F_R^2}
\frac{k^2/a^2}{k^2/a^2+m_R^2}.
\label{eq:eta-form}
\end{equation}
These formulae are not the perturbation equations of the failed hard-multiplier action. That action contains two scalar combinations, kinetic mixing, a trace-matter coupling and an indefinite kinetic signature. The equations above are retained only to specify what a future ghost-free one-scalar successor must derive from its own quadratic action; they are not predictions of Eq.~\eqref{eq:ibp-action}.

The tensor quadratic action is
\begin{equation}
S_T^{(2)}=\frac18\int\dd t\dd^3x\,a^3M_{\rm Pl}^2F
\left[\dot h_{ij}^2-\frac{(\nabla h_{ij})^2}{a^2}\right].
\label{eq:tensorlocked}
\end{equation}
For the pure gravitational tensor sector on a homogeneous background, and assuming no additional anisotropic tensor coupling from the matter interaction,
\begin{equation}
\boxed{Q_T=M_{\rm Pl}^2F>0,\qquad c_T^2=1.}
\label{eq:tensor-gates}
\end{equation}
The scalar quadratic action must have
\begin{equation}
S_s^{(2)}=\int\dd t\dd^3x\,a^3Q_s
\left[\dot\zeta^2-c_s^2\frac{(\nabla\zeta)^2}{a^2}\right],
\end{equation}
with
\begin{equation}
\boxed{Q_s>0,\qquad c_s^2>0.}
\label{eq:scalar-stability}
\end{equation}
The current calculation verifies $F>0$, $Z>0$ and $c_T^2=1$ on the integrated branch. It also shows that the hard-multiplier scalar Hessian is indefinite. It does not replace the required ADM scalar perturbation calculation.
